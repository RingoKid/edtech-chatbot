## Lesson Plan K: Sentence Structure — Be-Verbs (Weather/Temperature)

Language Construction Sample Lesson Grade K

**Input:**
- Unit focus: Weather and temperature
- Vocabulary words: weather, temperature, thermometer, chilly, freezing
- Genre: Informational
- Sentence structure focus: Be-verbs
- Previously learned structure: Action verbs; putting a "y" at the end of a noun to turn it into an adjective (describing word)
- Grade: K
- Ability level: On target

### A. Standards

K.P.AC.2.c — Make decisions about sentence structure and syntax in order to accommodate and influence the audience and achieve a specific purpose.

K.L.GC.2.a — Recognize that conventional sentences always include a subject and a predicate.

K.L.GC.2.b — With adult support, use simple sentences to express complete thoughts in written texts.

### B. Objectives

- Determine the difference between action verb and be-verb sentences
- Construct sentences using be-verbs to tell about the temperature
- Create a picture depicting the temperature
- Describe the temperature using a be-verb sentence and adjective

### C. Materials

- Picture of sunny weather when the temperature is hot and one when it is cold
- Noun pictures of wind, sun, rain, snow, fog, storm, and ice
- Picture of a thermometer
- Thermometer and temperature picture cut-outs for children
- Composing paper
- Whiteboards (optional)

### D. Scripted Language Construction Lesson

**1. Review & Connect**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to quickly review vocabulary from a unit of study and specific sentence structures children have used to express those concepts and vocabulary. Do not introduce new vocabulary or concepts. The goal is to have children apply vocabulary that they have been directly taught using sentence structures."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We have been talking about what?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "the weather."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we look at this first picture, what is the weather outside? Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "sunny."

Corrective Feedback: Some children may say "hot," confusing the temperature for the weather.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Hot is not the weather. Remember the weather is what we can see. Everyone look at the sky in this picture. What do you see?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "Sun."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You see the sun, and the sun shines down on us. Do you see rain coming down? Do you see clouds moving and covering the sun? Do you see wind moving the trees?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond no.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If the sun is out, then we can say that the weather is ___"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "sunny."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, it is sunny. We talked about how we can put a 'y' at the end of some nouns and it says /e/ at the end. That turns it into a describing word. Let's do our **Change-the-Word**."

👉 **Teacher Does:** In this activity the teacher provides noun picture cards for each of the sentences for children to see that the noun changes into an adjective.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we saw wind moving things around, we would say, the weather is ______."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "windy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "windy" next to the wind noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we saw lots of clouds in the sky and they were covering the sun, we would say, the weather is ____?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "cloudy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "cloudy" next to the cloud noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we saw rain coming down, we would say, the weather is ______."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "rainy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "rainy" next to the rain noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we see snow falling from the clouds, it is ___"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "snowy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "snowy" next to the snow noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we see fog all around and it is hard to see, it is ___"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "foggy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "foggy" next to the fog noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we see a storm with dark clouds, lightning, and heavy rain, it is ___"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "stormy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "stormy" next to the storm noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If we see ice on the ground and things are frozen, it is ___"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "icy."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes "icy" next to the ice noun card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's look at this next picture. What is the weather here?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should say "sunny."

Corrective Feedback: Some children may incorrectly say that it is "cold" or "snowy."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You're right that it is probably cold because we can see snow on the ground, but remember cold and hot are what we feel. The weather is what we see happening, like the sun shining or the rain pouring or the wind moving. That's important because it can help people understand if the weather could be dangerous and how to prepare. So here we do see snow on the ground, but is it snowing in this picture? So the snow must have happened before. What's happening right now in the picture? The sun is shining. So, the weather is ____?"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "In both of these two pictures the weather is sunny, but the temperature is different. It is not the same. What is the temperature everyone?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "what you feel."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, it's what our bodies feel when the outside air touches it. If you went outside in this first picture, what would the air feel like?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "hot."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "It would be hot. What about in the next picture, what would you feel?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "cold."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, and how do we know it's cold? One way we know it's cold is because we see snow. Snow is rainwater that has frozen. And the air has to be a specific temperature for that to happen. What tool do we use to measure the temperature of the air? Remember it gives a number, letting us know how hot or how cold the air is."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "thermometer."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Thermometer, yes. Everyone say /th/ — thermometer. You hear the /th/ sound in thermometer. The thermometer is the special tool used to measure the temperature of the air. It gives a number, and who remembers what number it needs to be for rain to turn into snow?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "32."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Below 32 — so it's very cold, or what's that other word?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "freezing."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Freezing, good."

**2. Model, Explain, Engage (10 minutes)**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section is to introduce and reinforce a targeted sentence structure through modeling and providing clear examples, while connecting to genre purpose and incorporating children's vocabulary associated with what they are reading and writing about in their unit of study."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's remember that when we are writing, we have to talk about a main something. It can be a person, place, thing, or animal. What's that called, everyone?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "the subject."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The subject. So, we first have to name what we are talking about — the subject, the namer. But then we have to tell the audience what our special something is doing, so we need an _____."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "action."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We need an action, the action verb of the sentence. But we also said sometimes we don't need to tell our audience what our main something is doing. Sometimes we just want to tell our audience something special about our subject, our main something. Be-verbs help us tell something about our subject — what they are. They aren't doing anything. We are just telling something about that subject, the main thing we are talking about."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's look at these pictures from our book about gardens. Here's our garden snake. Now what can a snake do?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "slither."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, a snake slithers. My subject, my namer, is what?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "snake."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "And what did my snake, my subject, do?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "slithers."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Where did he slither?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "in the garden."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Say the whole thing."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "The snake slithers in the garden."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now what if I want to just tell you something special or something important or some information about this snake? What if the snake was chasing mice through the garden? We could say that the snake is ______."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "hungry."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Hungry — the snake is hungry. What else could we say?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child: "The snake is mean."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "He may be mean because he wants to eat."

💬 **Child Response:** Child: "The snake is poisonous."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Poisonous — good word. Tell everyone what that means."

💬 **Child Response:** Response will vary.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, some snakes have poison. If they bite you, the poison can go into your body, which can really hurt you. But the poison is usually used to sting other animals they want to hunt and eat — their prey. Sometimes they use their poison on other predators, the animals that are after them and want to eat them."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So, the snake is hungry; the snake is mean; the snake is poisonous. Did our snake do anything?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "no."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "No, we are just saying something about the snake, not what it is doing. We use a special type of verb. It is not an action verb — it is a be-verb because it does not show action; it tells us something about the snake, something about its being. If we look here, we have 'The snake slithers' — the action is 'slithers.' But look at these sentences: 'The snake is… The snake is… The snake is…' They all say the snake is ___ something. So what be-verb is being repeated to tell us these different things about the snake?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes — 'is' — that is a be-verb. It is the verb in the sentence that tells us something about our subject, not what our subject did."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Take out your whiteboards. First, let's spell our be-verb 'is.' Let's stretch it out. Is — /i/ — /s/. How many sounds?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "Two." Children draw two lines side by side.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me — how many letters should you have?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "Two."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Two, one for each sound. Is — what's the first sound?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "/i/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Spell it. Is — what's the second sound?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "/s/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Spell it. Read me the letters."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "i-s."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What does it spell?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "Is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good. Now get ready. We are going to do our **Sentence-Hunt** Game. I am going to read a sentence from our book. You are listening for whether that sentence is a be-verb sentence or if it is an action verb sentence. If it is a be-verb sentence, you should hear 'is.' 'The snake is poisonous.' If you hear 'is' and the sentence is just telling something special or important about your subject, your namer, then you will write 'is' on your whiteboard. That shows that the sentence has the same be-verb pattern we are looking for. If the sentence is an action sentence, like 'The snake slithers in the garden,' you will write an X to show that it is not a be-verb sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Watch me. My turn. My sentence is: The ladybug eats aphids. My subject is 'ladybug' — that's my namer, the thing I am talking about. I am thinking: what did the ladybug do? It eats, so I have an action. I also did not hear 'is,' so I am going to write an X. This is not a be-verb sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay, ready. My turn. My sentence is: The tomato is ripe. Is this an action or a be-verb sentence?"

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher waits for children to write "is" or an X on their whiteboards.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me. Yes, this is a be-verb sentence. The tomato is not doing anything. We are just saying something about the tomato — it is ripe. And I heard the special be-verb. What is that be-verb you wrote?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Is — yes."

📝 **Example Bank:** Example sentences from _Up in the Garden and Down in the Dirt_:

_Action:_
- Seeds sprout from the soil.
- Worms wiggle through the soil.
- Bees carry pollen to the flowers.
- The roly-polies crawl under leaves.

_Be-Verbs:_
- The tomato is ripe.
- The flower is beautiful.
- The soil is moist.
- The ant is beneath the soil.
- The sun is bright.

**4. Guided Practice**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section of the lesson is to guide children in applying the targeted sentence structure to express an idea that relates to topic and genre, incorporating their vocabulary words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you are going to be a weather person and tell everyone what the temperature is. What's this again, everyone?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "thermometer."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Thermometer — it measures how hot or cold it is outside. It gives a number, and we can then describe that temperature. All of you have your cut-out thermometers with numbers that tell us the degrees outside. Look at the top here — the largest number, 100 degrees. So, we can describe 80–100 degrees as being _____."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "hot."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, hot. So, we have hot, warm, cool, chilly, cold, freezing. We are going to do our **Build-the-Sentence** game using our be-verb 'is.'"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you each have picture cards showing one of these temperatures outside. Let's look at the first picture. Put this picture in front of you. What do we see in this picture, Lisa?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child: "The boy is drinking water."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "He's drinking water. When do we drink water? When we are thirsty. What do you think is making him thirsty? Yes, the sun is bright and hot, and we see that he is sweating. So, what is the temperature, everyone?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "Hot."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Say the whole thing."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "The temperature is hot."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now Partner 1, look at the next picture. Tell Partner 2 what you see in the picture."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher waits and observes conversations.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Partner 2, tell Partner 1 what the temperature is."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides wait time.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Partner 1, do you agree? Give me a thumbs up."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher ensures agreement. If there is a thumbs down, the teacher and children discuss why — for example, the difference between freezing and cold.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is the temperature, everyone? Say the whole thing."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "The temperature is chilly."

**Application**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section of the lesson is to provide children further practice using the new sentence structure to express their ideas and incorporate vocabulary from a unit of study."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay, now we are going to do our **Sentence-Imitation** write; you are going to come up with your own be-verb sentence. First, you are going to draw a picture about one of these temperatures and write what the temperature is. Notice on your writing paper, the top has a box for your drawing. Under the box are your handwriting lines. Look what is also there — there are four lines. That means you are going to write four words: 'The temperature is ______.' You get to choose if the temperature is hot, warm, cool, chilly, cold, or freezing. Remember, I need to see details in your picture which show your temperature outside just like in the pictures we looked at. If it's chilly, are you going to show yourself in a pool?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "no."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If it is freezing, are you going to be playing outside?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "no."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "If it is warm, are you going to have a hat and gloves?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "no."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, everyone pick one of the pictures showing a temperature. Put everything else away. Get started. Draw your picture. I am going to put 5 minutes on the clock."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher circulates during the five minutes providing feedback.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Partner 1, tell Partner 2 about your picture."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides 30 seconds.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Partner 2, tell Partner 1 about your picture."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides 30 seconds.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Partner 1, tell Partner 2 what your temperature is. Partner 2, tell Partner 1 what your temperature is. Does everyone know their temperature?"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay, now you will write your be-verb sentence telling us about the temperature: 'The temperature is ______.' Four words. Okay, first word — go, everyone. 'The' is a sight word — we should know this. Show me."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Children show their paper with the first word completed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Next: The temperature. Now that's a big word, so you have to spell it as best you can — what do you hear? /t/ — write as much as you can hear. Show me."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Children show their paper with the second word completed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The temperature is — we know 'is.' Stretch it."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "/i/ /s/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Spell it."

💬 **Child Response:** Children: "i-s."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay, write it."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Children write "is" and show their paper with the third word completed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The temperature is — go ahead and write your word that describes the temperature. Listen to the sounds you hear in your word, and write all the sounds you hear. Show me."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Children show their paper with the fourth word completed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Lovely!"

**5. Wrap-Up**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section is to remind children about the target structure, how it's different from previously learned structures and why they are important to the type of genre you are reading and writing in class."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone, we are working on writing informational text, we want to give information to the audience. Be-verb sentences tell us something about our subject. Action sentences tell us about what they do. We need both kinds of sentences so that we can tell the audience some facts, some things that are true about our topic. I am going to give you a sentence, you tell me if it is action — for an action verb sentence — or being — for a be-verb sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The rain poured from the clouds."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should chorally respond "Action-Verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Very good. What action verb did I use?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "poured."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Excellent — 'poured.' That is my action-verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The weather is windy. Get Ready. Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should answer "Be-Verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! What was that special be-verb I used?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Is — perfect. Kiss your brains."
