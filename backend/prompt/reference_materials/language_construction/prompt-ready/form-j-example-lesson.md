Vocabulary & Sentence Structure Sample Lesson Grade K

### A. Standards

K.P.AC.2.c — Make decisions about sentence structure and syntax in order to accommodate and influence the audience and achieve a specific purpose.

K.L.GC.2.a — Recognize that conventional sentences always include a subject and a predicate.

K.L.GC.2.c — With adult support, use singular and plural subjects with matching verbs.

K.L.V.3.e — Use knowledge of word relationships and learned vocabulary words and phrases when making word choices in speaking and writing.

### B. Objectives

- Identify be-verbs in a sentence
- Differentiate between action verbs and be-verbs
- Construct simple be-verb sentences using the verbs "is" and "are"
- Apply vocabulary words (e.g., fierce, nimble) in an informational sentence

### C. Materials

- Visual cards of sea creatures (e.g., shark, dolphin, sea turtle, squid, orca)
- Vocabulary picture cards (predator, prey, fierce, nimble)
- Sentence cards with be-verbs (is, are)
- Noun marker cards ("a" and "the" in uppercase and lowercase)
- Sticky notes (for periods and writing f/n for vocabulary)
- Sight word chart
- Sentence-building cards or pocket chart (optional support)
- Whiteboard and markers (for teacher modeling)
- Laminated sentence starter cards or student mats
- Timer (for the Add-a-Word challenge)

### D. Scripted Vocabulary & Sentence Structure Lesson

**1. Review & Connect (5 minutes)**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to quickly review vocabulary from a unit of study and specific sentence structures children have used to express those concepts and vocabulary. Do not introduce new vocabulary or concepts. The goal is to have children apply vocabulary that they have been directly taught using sentence structures."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yesterday, we were talking about the what?"

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to a visual cue.

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "ocean habitat."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "And we said that a habitat is a what?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond in some way that a habitat is a home.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, it's the place where animals and plants live. It gives them what they need to grow and stay alive — like food, water, air, and shelter. Every animal and plant has special body parts or ways of doing things that help them live in their habitat. In habitats, we have predators and prey."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher draws children's attention to visual for predator and prey.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember, what's a predator? Predators _____"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "hunt and eat other animals."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "They are usually larger animals, and they have to eat smaller animals. Prey are the animals that are being what?, everyone _____"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "hunted."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Prey usually have to be fast, and they have to hide. Some have body parts that help them hide. Some sea creatures can be both predator and prey. Like the squid. The squid hunts for fish; it is a predator. Remember they use their tentacles to catch prey. But, dolphins, whales, and sharks can also hunt and eat squid. So, squid can also be prey."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher shows a picture of an Orca, or another predator, especially a picture that reveals its sharp teeth.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's look at this ocean animal. Is this animal a predator or is it prey?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should say predator.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Why do you think this is a predator and not prey?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should point out that the teeth are sharp for eating other animals.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, an Orca uses its sharp teeth to be able to eat other sea creatures, like a seal. So, an Orca can live in the ocean because its body allows it to be able to eat and survive there."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Before we said every sentence needs a subject, that is the person, animal, thing, or place that we are talking about. The subject is the star of the show. Every sentence needs a subject and an action verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's play our **Spotlight-Sentence**. I am going to tell you a sentence about one of the creatures in the sea and you are going to tell me the subject — the who or what sea creature that I am talking about? Watch me because I am going to show you how to do this. So, here's the first picture, and my sentence is 'Manatees nibble on seagrass.' So, I am thinking: What is the subject? The subject is who or what the sentence is about? In my sentence 'manatees nibble seagrass,' it is telling me about manatees, that is what my sentence is about. And, manatees are the ones doing the action. They nibble. So, the subject is manatee."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. My sentence is 'Whales gulp fish in the ocean.'"

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays sentence along with the visual of the whale in the subject spot of the sentence.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is the subject? Who/What is this sentence all about? Get Ready. Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "whales."

📝 **Example Bank:** Additional examples about sea creatures and what they eat for use in this review (subjects bolded):

- **Dolphins** catch fish with their mouth.
- **Sea turtles** munch on seaweed.
- **Belugas** chomp shrimp.
- **Narwhals** slurp up crabs on the ocean floor.
- **Whales** gulp fish in the ocean.

**2. Model, Explain, Engage (10 minutes)**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section is to introduce and reinforce a targeted sentence structure through modeling and providing clear examples, while connecting to genre purpose and incorporating children's vocabulary associated with what they are reading and writing about in their unit of study."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great! So we know that a sentence needs a subject and a verb. Verbs are usually actions. But, remember we are writing informational text. Sometimes we need to tell information about our subject. Like, an Orca is a predator."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays the sentence.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Did the Orca do anything?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond no.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "No. We are just telling something about the Orca's being — or something that is about who they are as a sea creature. So these are special verbs. They are not action verbs."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "They are 'be' verbs: The Orca is a predator."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher circles/underlines the word "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I could also say the Orcas are predators."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher circles the word "are."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Why did I use 'are'?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond that there is more than one.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. We use the verb 'are' when talking about more than one person, place, thing, or animal."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays the words "is" and "are."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So, these two special be-verbs are used to tell information about the subject — the who or what we are talking about."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let me show you some more examples."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays example sentences, calling children's attention to the purpose of the sentence and the be-verb used for that purpose.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Sofia is a good listener. In this sentence I want to tell you something special about Sophia. She didn't do anything. I am just telling you something about her. She's a good listener."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What verb did I use to tell you she's a good listener? Think about our special be-verbs."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "How about this sentence: Cal and Floyd are my students. Did Cal and Floyd do anything?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond no.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "No, they didn't, but I want to tell you that they are my friends. What verb did I use to tell you they're my friends? Think about our special be-verbs."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "are."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Why did I use 'are' and not 'is'?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond that there is more than one person as the subject.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly. I am going to show some be-verb sentences. You tell me if the be-verb I used is 'is' or 'are.'"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. Listen. Whales are mammals. Get Ready. What's the verb?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children answer "are."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right! I want to say that all whales are mammals, not just one. And if I am talking about more than one whale, how did I change the word 'whale'?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should notice that there is an s on the end.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly right. When we put an s at the end of a noun, it changes it into more than one."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides a few more examples.

**4. Guided Practice**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section of the lesson is to guide children in applying the targeted sentence structure to express an idea that relates to topic and genre, incorporating their vocabulary words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you are going to do **Build-a-Sentence**. You are going to tell me information about a sea creature. You are thinking about whether that sea creature is a predator or prey. You want to explain that a particular sea creature is a predator or prey in their ocean habitat."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I am going to give everybody a special be-verb card; the 'is' card. Everyone also has the predator and prey card, along with your 'noun marker' cards, 'a' and 'the'."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look up here at our sight words."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher draws children's attention to the sight word chart.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We know that 'the' and 'a' are words that come where _____"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should say "before a noun."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, they mark a noun. We also know that they can be capital or uppercase if we start a sentence, and lowercase if they do not start a sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to pick a subject. The subject of your sentence will be about one of these sea creatures."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays pictures of different sea creatures children have learned about.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to create a sentence that shares information about whether your sea creature is a predator or prey. Watch me."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I picked the sea turtle. I know that sea turtles eat seaweed, and that bigger animals in the ocean, like the orca, can eat the sea turtle. So I think it is a prey."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's look at our cards to build this sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So I am thinking: The sea turtle is a prey. First, I need to decide whether 'the' should be upper case or lowercase. It's starting my sentence, so I pick uppercase 'The.' I am going to place my uppercase 'The' card at the beginning. Then, I am going to put my sea creature, my subject — sea turtle — The sea turtle. Now I want to say something about the sea turtle, not what he does. So, I need my special be-verb card 'is'. My sea turtle is 'a' — I put my noun marker card next. And now I tell everyone what my sea turtle is — a prey."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher reads the sentence back pointing to each card — "The sea turtle is a prey."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What do I need at the end of my sentence?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should answer "period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. So, I take my sticky note, and dot, for the period. Now everyone knows that is the end of my sentence. The sea turtle is a prey, period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it's your turn. Everyone choose your topic, the sea creature that is the subject of your sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone hold up your subject so I can see."

💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up a sea creature card that they have learned about.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Find your noun marker card. Decide if you should use uppercase or lowercase 'the.'"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Casey, what's your subject?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds with "the dolphin."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The dolphin. Did you use your uppercase or lowercase card?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds uppercase.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. You are starting a sentence, so you use uppercase."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher takes one or two more examples.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, everyone check that you have uppercase 'The' in front of your sea creature."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So, what's next? We want to say something about the sea creature. What verb can do that?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now tell me what your creature is: a predator or prey? Make sure you mark your noun and use your sticky note for the period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Jasper, tell everyone what be-verb sentence you made."

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds, "The walrus is a predator, period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect. Did the walrus do an action?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds no.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So what kind of a verb did you use?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond "be-verb."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher has multiple children read their sentences.

**Application**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section of the lesson is to provide children further practice using the new sentence structure to express their ideas and incorporate vocabulary from a unit of study."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright everyone, the last thing you are going to do is pick another sea creature and create a be-verb sentence to tell me whether your animal is a predator or a prey. This time I want you to add one of our vocabulary words. We are doing the **Add-A-Word** challenge."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "After you create the sentence, choose a vocabulary word to describe the predator or prey."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look up here."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher directs children's attention to two vocabulary words with a visual.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We have Fierce and Nimble. So you are thinking to yourself, 'How would I describe my predator or how would I describe my prey. Would it be fierce or nimble?'"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "To show me which word you chose, you are going to write either the letter f for /f/ fierce, or you will write the letter n for /n/ nimble. You are writing that on your post-it and adding that to your sentence. I am going to put five minutes on the clock. When I say stop, everyone puts their hands up. Get Ready. Go."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher gives children five minutes to create their sentence, adding in an adjective vocabulary word. Teacher monitors to support students who may need extra support.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay stop. Hands up. Hands down."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Cooper, tell us your sentence."

💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond: "The shark is a fierce predator."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Why would a predator be fierce?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond that the predator has to hunt and fight to get its food.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, the predator has to fight to eat, so usually predators have to be fierce. Fierce means strong, powerful, or full of energy. A predator isn't going to give up because they need to eat to survive."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues to take examples, writing them or displaying them for children to see, drawing attention to vocabulary and the be-verb "is."

**5. Wrap-Up**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this section is to remind children about the target structure, how it's different from previously learned structures and why they are important to the type of genre you are reading and writing in class."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone, we are working on writing informational text, we want to give information to the audience. Be-verb sentences tell us something about our subject. Action sentences tell us about what they do. We need both kinds of sentences so that we can tell the audience some facts, some things that are true about our topic. I am going to give you a sentence, you tell me if it is action — for an action verb sentence — or being — for a be-verb sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The manatee is the sloth of the sea. Get Ready. Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should chorally respond "Be-Verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Very good. What special verb did I use?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Excellent — 'is.' That is my be-verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The manatee munches slowly on seagrass. Get Ready. Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Children should answer "Action."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! 'Munch' is to take your time eating something. It's an action. You all did awesome."
