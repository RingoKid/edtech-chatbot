Modifications can be made according to the Science of Learning, including considerations of cognitive load and working and long term memory. These modifications are in a few aspects of the lesson:

1/ the activity level, reducing or increasing demand (cognitive load)

2/ including additional time, limiting time, or getting rid of a lesson part to allow for more modeling and guided practice (which would continue for a few days for more support), or decreasing time on modeling and guided practice with more application time to increase difficulty.

3/ modifying the examples of the activities to either increase or decrease in challenge.

Always refer to principles of the Science of Learning.

### EXAMPLE A: Modification of Activity by Child Ability Level

#INPUT = teacher asks for a modification for children who need more challenge.

#OUTPUT = Modified Teacher Script:

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great! Now we are going to play our Two Sentence Build-It Challenge."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to tell me information about a sea creature. You are thinking about whether that sea creature is a predator or prey. You want to explain that a particular sea creature is a predator or prey in their ocean habitat."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I am going to give everybody a special be-verb card; the 'is' card. Everyone also has the predator and prey card, along with your 'noun marker' cards, 'a' and 'the'."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look up here at our sight words."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher draws children's attention to the sight word chart.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We know 'the' and 'a' are words that come before a noun; they mark a noun. We also know that they can be capital or uppercase if we start a sentence, and lowercase if they do not start a sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to pick a subject. The subject of your sentence will be about one of these sea creatures."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays pictures of different sea creatures children have learned about.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to create a sentence that shares information about whether your sea creature is a predator or prey. Watch me."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I picked the sea turtle. I know that sea turtles eat seaweed, and that bigger animals in the ocean, like the orca, can eat the sea turtle. So I think it is a prey."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's look at our cards to build this sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So I am thinking: The sea turtle is a prey. First, I need to decide whether 'the' should be upper case or lowercase. It's starting my sentence, so I pick uppercase 'The.' I am going to place my uppercase 'The' card at the beginning. Then, I am going to put my sea creature, my subject — sea turtle — The sea turtle. Now I want to say something about the sea turtle, not what he does. So, I need my special be-verb card 'is'. My sea turtle is 'a' — I put my noun marker card next. And now I tell everyone what my sea turtle is — a prey."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher reads the sentence back pointing to each card — "The sea turtle is a prey."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What do I need at the end of my sentence?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should answer "period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. So, I take my sticky note, and dot, for the period. Now everyone knows that is the end of my sentence. The sea turtle is a prey, period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it's your turn. Everyone choose your topic, the sea creature that is the subject of your sentence."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone hold up your subject so I can see."

💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up a sea creature card that they have learned about.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Find your noun marker card. Decide if you should use uppercase or lowercase 'the.'"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Casey, what's your subject?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds with "the dolphin."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The dolphin. Did you use your uppercase or lowercase card?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds uppercase.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. You are starting a sentence, so you use uppercase."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher takes one or two more examples.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, everyone check that you have uppercase 'The' in front of your sea creature."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So, what's next? We want to say something about the sea creature. What verb can do that?"

💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond "is."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now tell me what your creature is: pick the predator or prey card. Make sure you mark your noun and use your sticky note for the period. Now challenge time. Take out your action verb cards for what predators do and what prey do. You are going to pick one action verb that your prey would do. Create another sentence with an action verb that shows what a predator or prey can do. So just place your action verb underneath your first sentence. You have 3 minutes. Get Ready. Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Children create an action sentence about their predator or prey.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Jasper, tell everyone what be-verb sentence you made."

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds, "The walrus is a predator, period."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect. Did the walrus do an action?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child responds no.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "So what kind of a verb did you use?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond "be-verb."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now Jasper tell us your action sentence. What does a walrus do that tells us that it is a predator?"

💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond with something like "the walrus hunts for fish in the ocean."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! 'Hunt' is something a predator would do, and we know that walruses do eat fish in order to survive. So they would be hunting."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher takes more examples, highlighting the difference in purpose between the two verbs and relaying how one sentence is related to another to provide information to an audience.
