## FORM C: K Example Output

This is Form C.

Input:
Objective = letter sound and writing letter b (beginning and ending CVC words)
Grade = K

Output:

Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

A. Standards

K.F.PA.6.a Isolate and pronounce initial, final, and then medial sounds in spoken words with three phonemes
K.F.P.1.a Identify and produce basic one-to-one phoneme-grapheme correspondences for the most frequently used sound for each consonant.
K.F.H.1.a Form all uppercase and lowercase letters.
K.F.P.3.a Use knowledge of letter/sound correspondences to connect letters (graphemes) to sounds (phonemes) to encode simple one-syllable words, including high-frequency words (VC, CV, CVC).

B. Objectives
Isolate and identify the initial and final /b/ sound in CVC words
Connect the letter b to its phoneme /b/
Write the letter b with accuracy
Apply knowledge of the /b/ sound and its grapheme to encode CVC words

C. Materials
- Ball sound cards (for gesture-based cues)
- Sound wall or letter-sound anchor chart
- Mini whiteboards and dry-erase markers
- Elkonin boxes with beginning/ending position cards
- Pencils and handwriting paper
- Stretch-Sound handwriting paper with three boxes per word
- Vowel display cards (a, e, i, o)

**1. Review & Connect**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds. Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's review our sounds. We are doing our *Sound Sparks* game. Look up here. When I point to a sound. You will all say that sound when I say go, and when you see my hand close to a fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /a/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /a/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues this process, reviewing previous sounds; sounds to preview to support later activities of the lesson = p, d, t, c, l, g, n, m, j; short a, e, i, o.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Here's your challenge. Now we are doing *Write that Sound.* I say the sound. You write the sound and show me when I say show me, and you see my hand close to fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My sound is /a/. Write the sound. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up their whiteboards together.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My sound is /d/. Write the sound. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up their whiteboards together.

Corrective Feedback: Some children may confuse the direction of d, writing instead b or p.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are close. My sound is /d/. Everyone look up here. Some of you wrote this."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes b.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "This was our new sound we learned yesterday. This sound is /b/. We are writing /d/. This is /d/."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes d on the board.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is different about these two letters? Everyone think. Partner 1 tell Partner 2. Stop. Partner 2 tell Partner 1. Stop. Jesslyn, what is different about the /b/ and /d/?"
💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond that bottom bubble is different.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, both the /d/ and /b/ have a straight line down, but one has a bubble on the back, the left, and one has a bubble in the front, on the right. Everyone say /d/. Where's the bubble, on the front or back? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** On the back.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone say /b/. Where's the bubble? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** On the front.

**2. Model, Explain, Engage**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We've been working on the /b/ sound. Say the sound."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do our sound motion. Get Ready Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children make a two-hand grab, then throw forward, saying /b/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We can hear /b/ at the beginning of words like ball, but we can also hear /b/ at the end of words like cab."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do **Stretch the Sounds**, so we can practice how to listen for /b/ at the beginning or end."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My word is *big*. Stretch the Sounds."
💬 **Child Response:** Children say each sound /b/ /i/ /g/, while holding up a finger for every sound, then repeat the word — *big*.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My word is cab. Stretch the Sounds."
💬 **Child Response:** Children say each sound /c/ /a/ /b/, while holding up a finger for every sound, then repeat the word — cab.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides 4-5 words. CVC words to use: big, bin, bob, bog, bat, bed, bib, cab, job, lab, web, rib, cob.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we are going to play our **Sound Hunt Game.** You are going to stretch those sounds to hunt for the /b/ sound. You are thinking, 'Do you hear it at the beginning, or do you hear it at the end?' Each of you has a ball card because ball starts with the /b/ sound. You also have two boxes, one beginning box and one ending box."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to elkonin box on left for beginning and right for the ending.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We are listening for where the /b/ sound is at, the beginning or ending of the word. I will say a word, and if you hear the /b/ sound at the beginning, I want you to slide the ball into the beginning box. If you hear the /b/ sound at the end of the word, I want you to slide the ball into the ending spot."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay. Let's get started. My turn. The word is big. Get Ready. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have moved the ball card into the beginning box.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect. The word big b---i---g starts with a /b/ sound. I don't hear /b/ at the end of the word big; I hear it at the beginning. Great job."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright put your listening ears on. My turn. The word is *web*. Get Ready. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have moved their ball card into the ending box.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process to cement sound to memory then connects to letter form.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's think about writing the /b/ sound. The letter that matches the /b/ sound -- is the letter b. Watch me closely. I will show you how to form lowercase b. No one is using their pencil yet or writing. You are watching me."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates on a visual that shows children the correct sequence of letter strokes and uses sequence verbal cues.
✏️ **Verbal Cues for letter b:** Straight line down; put a bubble on it.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "First, start at the top line. Straight line down. Lift up. Now put a bubble on it."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats as needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Say it with me as you watch. Straight line down. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Put a bubble on it. Say it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you say it as I write. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat *straight line down, put on the belly* as teacher demonstrates the strokes.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats a few times.

**3. Guided Practice**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose is to give children practice writing the letter while supporting proper pencil grip and correct sequence of stroke formation using handwriting lines."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's practice how to write lowercase b. We are not using our pencils yet. Let's do our Magic Finger Write. Place your finger at the top of the line. Let me see."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks students finger placement.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember it's important when we are writing to try to touch the lines. The lines are there to help us. Straight line down."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger from top to bottom line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Put on a belly."
💬 **Child Response:** Children lift finger and add a bottom curve to the bottom.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Repeat as needed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now, pick up the pencil. Show me how you hold your pencil. Remember we slant our paper. Your free hand holds the paper."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do our **Say and Write.** Say the steps as you write. Everyone put your pencil at the top line on the left. You want to start here."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to top left-hand line. Teacher corrects students if needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. Say and write. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter as they say the verbal cues "Straight line down. Put on the belly."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you are ready for **Write and Sound**. You are going to write the letter b, but this time you say the sound /b/ as you write. Everyone put your pencil at the top line. Ready. Write and Sound. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter as they say the sound /b/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.

**4. Application**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to facilitate children's spelling of the letter or letter patterns when they hear the sound(s). This supports children's early coordination of matching sound to letter(s) for the purpose of spelling words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Boys and girls, remember stretch the sounds. Now I am going to say a word. You are going to stretch those sounds and spell the word. We are doing three sound words. Consonant-vowel-consonant. I am going to remind you of the vowels you know so far."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays the a, e, i, and o vowels.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Where are we going to hear these vowels, the beginning, middle, or end of the word? Everyone. Right. These are the vowels you hear in the middle of the word. We are going to do stretch and spell. You are going to stretch the sounds of word. Look at your Stretch-Sound handwriting paper."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher directs students' attention to the handwriting sheet.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "How many sounds will we hear if these are CVC words. Everyone. Yes. Three sounds. That's why we see three boxes for each sound. You first stretch the sounds, then write the letter for each sound. Let me show you. My turn. The word is cab. So, I first stretch the sounds, c-a-b, the beginning sound is /c/. I know that the /c/ sound is written with the letter c. I am going to write c in the first block. So, we have /c/-/a/. The /a/ sound is written with the letter a, so I write the letter a, remember that's our vowel, in the middle box. So, we have /c/-/a/-/b/. That's our new sound at the end. I am going to write the letter b for the /b/ sound in the last box. Okay. Let's try."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. My first word is bin. Stretch the sound everyone. First sound. Write the letter. Second sound. Write the letter. Third sound. Write the letter."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher uses the same list of words that were used in the Sound Hunt activity above. Teacher monitors students' answers providing corrective feedback.

**5. Wrap-Up**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to remind students what they were focused on and provide praise for their accomplishments."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, boys and girls I am so proud of you. What sound have we been working on in our spelling?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond /b/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! And what letter do we write for the /b/ sound."
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond "b".
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show and tell me how to write b."
💬 **Child Response:** Children air write with sequence verbal cues — e.g., straight line down, put on the belly.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! Kiss your brains."
