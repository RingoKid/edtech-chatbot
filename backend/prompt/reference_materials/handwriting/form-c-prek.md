## FORM C: PreK Example Output

This is Form C.

Input:
Objective = letter sound and writing letter z
Grade = PreK

Output:

Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

A. Standards

CLL6.4c: Isolates the initial (beginning) sounds in words with adult guidance.
CLL7.4a: With prompting and support, recognizes and names some upper and lower case letters of the alphabet.
CLL9.4d: Writes some letters of the alphabet.

B. Objectives
Pronounce the /z/ sound correctly
Isolate the /z/ sound at the beginning of spoken words. Write the corresponding letter z to represent the /z/ sound. Form uppercase and lowercase z using correct stroke sequence.

C. Materials
- Zebra sound cards (for sound hunt & gesture-based cues)
- Sound wall or letter-sound anchor chart
- Mini whiteboards and dry-erase markers
- Dotted handwriting worksheets (uppercase and lowercase z)
- Pencils and handwriting paper
- Visual chart showing correct letter formation with cues
- Letter sorting cards or letter manipulatives
- Sound-Letter Hunt worksheet with scaffolded visual supports

1. Review & Connect

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds. Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's review our sounds. We are doing our Sound Sparks game. Look up here. When I point to a sound. You will all say that sound when I say go, and when you see my hand close to a fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /a/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /a/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues this process, reviewing previous sounds.

2. Model, Explain, Engage

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We've been working on the /z/ sound. My turn. Listen to my sound /z/. Put your hand on your vocal box–now say the sound /z/."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Did you feel your vocal box?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond yes.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "That means you are using your voice, so we use our voice to make the /z/ sound. Where are your lips when you make the sound?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say something like opened.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, you part your lips and your mouth is spread. And where is your tongue?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say top.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, the top of your mouth and slightly behind your teeth. Let's do our sound motion. Get Ready Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children use the buzzing bee gesture while saying /z/.
🤚 **Gesture:** Move hand in a zigzag motion through the air while making the /z/ sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's try. My turn. /z/. Jenna, get ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Child pronounces /z/ sound.
👉 **Teacher Does:** The teacher has each child in the group demonstrate the correct pronunciation and provides correction if necessary; if this is a whole group lesson, the teacher has all children repeat first. Then, the teacher will call on a few children who may need extra support.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great! Now we are going to play our Sound Hunt Game. Each of you has a zebra card because zebra starts with the /z/ sound. We are listening for the /z/ sound at the beginning of the word. I will say a word, and if you hear the /z/ sound at the beginning, I want you to lift up your zebra card when I say go. If you don't hear it, do not lift up the card."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The word is zebra. Get Ready...Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have lifted the zebra card.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. Everyone should have lifted the card because zebra begins with /z/. Alright put your listening ears on. My turn. The word is tiger. Get Ready…Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should not have lifted their card.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process to cement sound to memory then connects to letter form.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! The letter that matches the /z/ sound – is the letter z. Watch me closely. I will show you how to form uppercase Z then lower case z. No one is using their pencil yet or writing. You are watching me."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates on a visual that shows children the correct sequence of letter strokes and uses sequence verbal cues.
✏️ **Verbal Cues for letter z:** Little line across. Slant down the stairs. Little line across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "First, start at the top line. Little line across. Next, slant down the stairs. And then a little line across. 1, 2, 3 lines. Say it with me as you watch."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Start at the top line. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across. Say it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Slant down the stairs. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's Count. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children count 1, 2, 3 lines.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process for lowercase z–drawing children's attention to where lowercase z starts, on the dotted middle line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is different from where I started my uppercase Z and where I started lower case z?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say at the top or middle line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, where I start the line. Uppercase Z starts at the top line. Lowercase z starts at the middle dotted line. What's the same about both these letters?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to notice that they are both formed with three lines that are the same.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. They both have the same three line strokes; one just starts at the top, the other starts at the middle dotted line."

3. Guided Practice

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose is to give children practice writing the letter while supporting proper pencil grip and correct sequence of stroke formation using handwriting lines."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's practice how to write uppercase letter z first. We are not using our pencils yet. Let's do our Magic Finger Write. Place your finger at the top of the line. Let me see."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks students finger placement.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember it's important when we are writing to try to touch the lines. The lines are there to help us. Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Slant down the stairs."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger in a slant downwards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger across.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher has students do that a few times and repeat for lowercase z.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now, pick up the pencil. Show me how you hold your pencil. Remember we slant our paper. Your free hand holds the paper. Let's do our Trace & Say. I have dotted lines for you to trace. We are doing uppercase Z first."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to uppercase Z.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Where do we start if we want to write uppercase Z?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say the top line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. Everyone put your pencil at the top line on the left. You want to start here."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to top left-hand line. Teacher corrects students if needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. Write and say. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write using dotted lines as guides and say the sequence verbal cues: "Little line across. Slant down the stairs. Little line across."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you do one using the dotted lines."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Do one more using the dotted lines."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors and repeats process for lowercase z.

4. Application

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to facilitate children's spelling of the letter or letter patterns when they hear the sound(s). This supports children's early coordination of matching sound to letter(s) for the purpose of spelling words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Boys and girls, I am going back to the /z/ Sound Hunt game, but now it's the Sound-Letter Hunt game. I am going to say a word. You are listening to the beginning sound of that word. You are going to write the letter of the sound you hear in the beginning of the word. Let me show you. My turn. The word is apple. So I am thinking the beginning sound of 'apple' is /a/. I know that the /a/ sound is written with the letter a. I am going to look at my handwriting Sound-Letter Hunt worksheet."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher directs students' attention to the handwriting sound-hunt worksheet. The worksheet displays some previously learned letter-sounds along with the new targeted letter-sound. The handout shows the proper way to write those letters and dotted lines for children to use as guides.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "I see letter a. Now I am going to look to find letter a on my paper. I am going to look at how to write the letter a. Then, I am going to use the dotted lines to write the letter a. Okay are you ready? Listen. My turn. The word is zebra. Get ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should hear the sound /z/ and write the letter z.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher uses the same list of words that were used in the Sound Hunt activity above. Teacher monitors students' answers providing corrective feedback.

5. Wrap-Up

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to remind students what they were focused on and provide praise for their accomplishments."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, boys and girls I am so proud of you. What sound have we been working on?"
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher holds up zebra sound card.
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond /z/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! And which of these letters say /z/?"
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides children with a choice.
💬 **Child Response:** Children can touch the correct letter that is displayed in small groups, or the teacher can call on a student to circle the correct letter for the whole class.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me how to write it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children air write with sequence verbal cues–e.g., little line across, slant down the stairs, little line across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! Kiss your brains."
