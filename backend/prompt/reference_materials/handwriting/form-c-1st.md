## FORM C: 1st Example Output

This is Form C.

Input:
Objective = long i sound and CVCe spelling pattern
Grade = 1st

Output:

Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

A. Standards

1.F.PA.6.b Distinguish between short and long vowel sounds in spoken one-syllable words.
1.F.P.1.b Identify and produce both long and short vowel sounds for A, E, I, O, U, including final -e and vowel digraph.
1.F.P.3.a Encode regularly spelled one-syllable words with a variety of spelling patterns (VC, CVC, CVCe, VCC, CCVC, CVCC, CCVCC), including high-frequency words.
1.F.H.2.b Build handwriting fluency by forming accurate letters and words with increasing speed.

B. Objectives
Identify the long i sound
Distinguish between long and short vowel sounds
Properly form letters in CVCe words with accuracy and speed
Determine the correct spelling pattern by isolating the medial vowel sound
Encode CVC and CVCe words accurately

C. Materials
- Whiteboards and dry erase markers
- Handwriting Hurdle worksheet
- Handwriting paper
- Pencils

**1. Review & Connect**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds. Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We've been learning about CVCe words. Let's do a quick warm-up. We are going to do **Write the Pattern**. I am going to say a word, you will spell it, on your whiteboards, and decide if the middle vowel sound is long or short."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. My word is cape. Write the pattern. Go."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides wait time for writing.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children display whiteboards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great. We can hear the long a sound in the middle. How many sounds. Stretch it. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children stretch the sounds.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "How many?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say three.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "There's three sounds, but I hear long a. That tells me there is a silent e at the end. /c/-/a/ (long)-/p/, so I write a silent e at the end."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Next word. Listen. My word is cap. Write the pattern. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children display whiteboards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "How many sounds. Stretch it. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children stretch the sounds.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "How many?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say three.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "There's three sounds, but I hear short a. That tells me this is a CVC word, so I don't add a silent e."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Next word. Listen. My word is tap. Write the pattern. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children display whiteboards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. Next word. Listen. My word is tape. Write the pattern. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children display whiteboards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Words to use in this activity: tap → tape, cap → cape, mad → made, can → cane, mat → mate, rat → rate, hat → hate.

**2. Model, Explain, Engage**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that was focused on during the reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now magic e can turn any short vowel into a long vowel. We just learned how magic *e* can abracadabra change short i into long i. What's the sound for long i everyone?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children pronounce long i.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's play **Sound Hunt** to see if you can hear it. I am going to say a word. You are listening to the middle vowel sound. When I say what's the sound, you will first say the middle vowel sound you hear. When I say, *is that our sound?* You give me a thumbs up if you hear long *i.* Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children show a thumbs up.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "If you hear short i, long a, or short a, you will put your thumb down. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children show a thumbs down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I am going to show you an example first. Listen. My turn. My word is bit. I am thinking about the middle vowel sound. What's the sound? The middle vowel sound I hear in b-/i/-t is /i/. The sound is /i/. Is that our sound? I am thinking /i/ does not sound like /i/ (long), so I am going to put my thumb down."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it's your turn. First Listen. My word is bite. What's my sound? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond with long i sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Is that my sound?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say yes.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. We hear /i/ in the middle of b/i/t/ (e)."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "My Turn. Listen. My word is date. What's my sound? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond with long a sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Is that my sound?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say no.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Words to use in this activity: cap → cape, tap → tape, mad → made, can → cane, kit → kite, rip → ripe, fin → fine, pin → pine, hid → hide.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's review how to write the letter i and the letter e. Look up here. You can see the handwriting lines. There is the top line, there's my middle, and there's the bottom."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Watch me. I start at the middle line. Where do I start? I make a straight line down and touch that bottom line. I lift up and put a dot at the top. Straight line down dot at the top. Now you say it as I write. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat *straight line down, dot at the top* as teacher demonstrates the strokes.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats a few times.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's remember how to write the letter e. Now, e is tricky because you have to start in the middle of the bottom line and the middle line. Watch me. Slide across. Around we go. Notice that when I go around, the line touches the middle line and comes around to the bottom line. Now you say it as I write. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat *Slide across. Around we go,* as teacher demonstrates the strokes.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats a few times.

**3. Guided Practice**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose is to give children practice writing the letter while supporting proper pencil grip and correct sequence of stroke formation using handwriting lines."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great. Now we are going to work on writing our letters so that we will be able to spell words with the CVCe pattern. We are going to do our **Disappearing Letter** activity. Look at your Handwriting Hurdle worksheet."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "You see on the left hand side the letters with their correct numbered handwriting strokes. Look at the first letter. What's the letter?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond letter a.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Letter a. How many strokes?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond two.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "First stroke."
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond circle.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "The first stroke is a circle. Where does it start?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond middle.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "At the middle dotted line. What's the second stroke?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond straight line down.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "The second stroke is a straight line down. Where does it start?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond middle.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "At the middle line."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are going to study the letter, the strokes and the order of strokes and where those start. You want to keep that memory of how to write the letter in your mind. When I say disappear, you cover the letter. I count two seconds and say Write the letter. You then must write the letter correctly. Then I say disappear. Now you cover both letters. I'll count to four and say Write the letter. Still keeping those letters covered, you will write the letter again. Then I say disappear again; you cover all letters. Now I count to six, and will say Write the letter. You write the letter in the last spot."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Are you ready. Look at line one. Get ready. Study the letter i."
💬 **Child Response:** Children study the numbered directions of the letter.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. Disappear."
💬 **Child Response:** Children cover the letter.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "One, Two. Write the letter."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter from memory.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look at line two going across. Ready. Disappear."
💬 **Child Response:** Children cover the letter.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "One, two, three, four. Write the letter."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter from memory.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look at line three going across. Ready. Disappear."
💬 **Child Response:** Children cover the letter.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "One, two, three, four, five, six. Write the letter."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter from memory.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Letters to use for this activity: a, i, e, c, f, l.

**4. Application**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to facilitate children's spelling of the letter or letter patterns when they hear the sound(s). This supports children's early coordination of matching sound to letter(s) for the purpose of spelling words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "I am going back to the Sound Hunt game, but now it's the Sound-Letter Hunt game. I am going to say a word. That word is either going to be a CVC word or a CVCe word. That means what do you really have to be listening to?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond vowel.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "The vowel. You have to think about whether the vowel is short or long. If the vowel is short, does it get a silent e?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond no.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We write the silent e when the vowel is?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond long.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We write the silent e when the vowel is long."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Are you ready. Listen. The word is cap. Write the word."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the word.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Spell the word aloud. Everyone. (c-a-p cap). Make the correction if you need to."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get ready. Listen. The word is cape. Write the word."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the word.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Spell the word aloud. Everyone. (c-a-p-e cape). Make the correction if you need to."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues using the same words from the Sound Hunt activity.

**5. Wrap-Up**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to remind students what they were focused on and provide praise for their accomplishments."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You all did such an awesome job! What pattern have we been working on?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond CVCe.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "And what was the long vowel sound?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond long i.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is the sound if I take away the silent e. Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** Children pronounce short i sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! Kiss your brains."
