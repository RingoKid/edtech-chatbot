## FORM A: Possible Objectives

FORM A
This is FORM A.

Scaffolded Verbs for Possible Objectives (listed in order from least to most complex):

Orient, Point, Match, Name, Identify, Pronounce, Discriminate, Repeat, Trace, Write, Count, Segment, Isolate, Compare, Sort, Delete, Add, Substitute, Blend, Build, Spell, Generate, Combine, Construct

Use these verbs when creating objectives to scaffold children toward mastery. The verbs progress from basic awareness (Orient, Point) through recognition (Match, Name, Identify), production (Pronounce, Trace, Write), analysis (Segment, Isolate, Compare, Sort), manipulation (Delete, Add, Substitute, Blend), and synthesis (Build, Spell, Generate, Combine, Construct).

## FORM C: PreK Example Output

This is Form C.

Input:
Objective = letter sound and writing letter z
Grade = PreK

Output:

Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

A. Standards

CLL6.4c: Isolates the initial (beginning) sounds in words with adult guidance.
CLL7.4a: With prompting and support, recognizes and names some upper and lower case letters of the alphabet.
CLL9.4d: Writes some letters of the alphabet.

B. Objectives
Pronounce the /z/ sound correctly
Isolate the /z/ sound at the beginning of spoken words. Write the corresponding letter z to represent the /z/ sound. Form uppercase and lowercase z using correct stroke sequence.

C. Materials
- Zebra sound cards (for sound hunt & gesture-based cues)
- Sound wall or letter-sound anchor chart
- Mini whiteboards and dry-erase markers
- Dotted handwriting worksheets (uppercase and lowercase z)
- Pencils and handwriting paper
- Visual chart showing correct letter formation with cues
- Letter sorting cards or letter manipulatives
- Sound-Letter Hunt worksheet with scaffolded visual supports

1. Review & Connect

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds. Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's review our sounds. We are doing our Sound Sparks game. Look up here. When I point to a sound. You will all say that sound when I say go, and when you see my hand close to a fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /a/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /a/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues this process, reviewing previous sounds.

2. Model, Explain, Engage

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We've been working on the /z/ sound. My turn. Listen to my sound /z/. Put your hand on your vocal box–now say the sound /z/."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Did you feel your vocal box?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should respond yes.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "That means you are using your voice, so we use our voice to make the /z/ sound. Where are your lips when you make the sound?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say something like opened.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right, you part your lips and your mouth is spread. And where is your tongue?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say top.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, the top of your mouth and slightly behind your teeth. Let's do our sound motion. Get Ready Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children use the buzzing bee gesture while saying /z/.
🤚 **Gesture:** Move hand in a zigzag motion through the air while making the /z/ sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's try. My turn. /z/. Jenna, get ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Child pronounces /z/ sound.
👉 **Teacher Does:** The teacher has each child in the group demonstrate the correct pronunciation and provides correction if necessary; if this is a whole group lesson, the teacher has all children repeat first. Then, the teacher will call on a few children who may need extra support.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Great! Now we are going to play our Sound Hunt Game. Each of you has a zebra card because zebra starts with the /z/ sound. We are listening for the /z/ sound at the beginning of the word. I will say a word, and if you hear the /z/ sound at the beginning, I want you to lift up your zebra card when I say go. If you don't hear it, do not lift up the card."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. The word is zebra. Get Ready...Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have lifted the zebra card.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. Everyone should have lifted the card because zebra begins with /z/. Alright put your listening ears on. My turn. The word is tiger. Get Ready…Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should not have lifted their card.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process to cement sound to memory then connects to letter form.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! The letter that matches the /z/ sound – is the letter z. Watch me closely. I will show you how to form uppercase Z then lower case z. No one is using their pencil yet or writing. You are watching me."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates on a visual that shows children the correct sequence of letter strokes and uses sequence verbal cues.
✏️ **Verbal Cues for letter z:** Little line across. Slant down the stairs. Little line across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "First, start at the top line. Little line across. Next, slant down the stairs. And then a little line across. 1, 2, 3 lines. Say it with me as you watch."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Start at the top line. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across. Say it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Slant down the stairs. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's Count. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children count 1, 2, 3 lines.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process for lowercase z–drawing children's attention to where lowercase z starts, on the dotted middle line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is different from where I started my uppercase Z and where I started lower case z?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to say at the top or middle line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, where I start the line. Uppercase Z starts at the top line. Lowercase z starts at the middle dotted line. What's the same about both these letters?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children expected to notice that they are both formed with three lines that are the same.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. They both have the same three line strokes; one just starts at the top, the other starts at the middle dotted line."

3. Guided Practice

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose is to give children practice writing the letter while supporting proper pencil grip and correct sequence of stroke formation using handwriting lines."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's practice how to write uppercase letter z first. We are not using our pencils yet. Let's do our Magic Finger Write. Place your finger at the top of the line. Let me see."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks students finger placement.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember it's important when we are writing to try to touch the lines. The lines are there to help us. Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Slant down the stairs."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger in a slant downwards.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Little line across."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger across.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher has students do that a few times and repeat for lowercase z.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now, pick up the pencil. Show me how you hold your pencil. Remember we slant our paper. Your free hand holds the paper. Let's do our Trace & Say. I have dotted lines for you to trace. We are doing uppercase Z first."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to uppercase Z.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Where do we start if we want to write uppercase Z?"
💬 **Child Response:** Children should say the top line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. Everyone put your pencil at the top line on the left. You want to start here."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to top left-hand line. Teacher corrects students if needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. Write and say. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write using dotted lines as guides and say the sequence verbal cues: "Little line across. Slant down the stairs. Little line across."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you do one using the dotted lines."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Do one more using the dotted lines."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors and repeats process for lowercase z.

4. Application

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to facilitate children's spelling of the letter or letter patterns when they hear the sound(s). This supports children's early coordination of matching sound to letter(s) for the purpose of spelling words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Boys and girls, I am going back to the /z/ Sound Hunt game, but now it's the Sound-Letter Hunt game. I am going to say a word. You are listening to the beginning sound of that word. You are going to write the letter of the sound you hear in the beginning of the word. Let me show you. My turn. The word is apple. So I am thinking the beginning sound of 'apple' is /a/. I know that the /a/ sound is written with the letter a. I am going to look at my handwriting Sound-Letter Hunt worksheet."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher directs students' attention to the handwriting sound-hunt worksheet. The worksheet displays some previously learned letter-sounds along with the new targeted letter-sound. The handout shows the proper way to write those letters and dotted lines for children to use as guides.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "I see letter a. Now I am going to look to find letter a on my paper. I am going to look at how to write the letter a. Then, I am going to use the dotted lines to write the letter a. Okay are you ready? Listen. My turn. The word is zebra. Get ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should hear the sound /z/ and write the letter z.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher uses the same list of words that were used in the Sound Hunt activity above. Teacher monitors students' answers providing corrective feedback.

5. Wrap-Up

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to remind students what they were focused on and provide praise for their accomplishments."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright, boys and girls I am so proud of you. What sound have we been working on?"
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher holds up zebra sound card.
💬 **Child Response:** Children respond /z/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect! And which of these letters say /z/?"
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides children with a choice.
💬 **Child Response:** Children can touch the correct letter that is displayed in small groups, or the teacher can call on a student to circle the correct letter for the whole class.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me how to write it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children air write with sequence verbal cues–e.g., little line across, slant down the stairs, little line across.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Wonderful! Kiss your brains."

## FORM C: K Example Output

This is Form C.

Input:
Objective = letter sound and writing letter b (beginning and ending CVC words)
Grade = K

Output:

Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

A. Standards

K.F.PA.6.a Isolate and pronounce initial, final, and then medial sounds in spoken words with three phonemes
K.F.P.1.a Identify and produce basic one-to-one phoneme-grapheme correspondences for the most frequently used sound for each consonant.
K.F.H.1.a Form all uppercase and lowercase letters.
K.F.P.3.a Use knowledge of letter/sound correspondences to connect letters (graphemes) to sounds (phonemes) to encode simple one-syllable words, including high-frequency words (VC, CV, CVC).

B. Objectives
Isolate and identify the initial and final /b/ sound in CVC words
Connect the letter b to its phoneme /b/
Write the letter b with accuracy
Apply knowledge of the /b/ sound and its grapheme to encode CVC words

C. Materials
- Ball sound cards (for gesture-based cues)
- Sound wall or letter-sound anchor chart
- Mini whiteboards and dry-erase markers
- Elkonin boxes with beginning/ending position cards
- Pencils and handwriting paper
- Stretch-Sound handwriting paper with three boxes per word
- Vowel display cards (a, e, i, o)

**1. Review & Connect**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds. Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's review our sounds. We are doing our *Sound Sparks* game. Look up here. When I point to a sound. You will all say that sound when I say go, and when you see my hand close to a fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /m/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. My Turn."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to /a/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Get Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children chorally respond /a/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher continues this process, reviewing previous sounds; sounds to preview to support later activities of the lesson = p, d, t, c, l, g, n, m, j; short a, e, i, o.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Here's your challenge. Now we are doing *Write that Sound.* I say the sound. You write the sound and show me when I say show me, and you see my hand close to fist."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My sound is /a/. Write the sound. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up their whiteboards together.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My sound is /d/. Write the sound. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children hold up their whiteboards together.

Corrective Feedback: Some children may confuse the direction of d, writing instead b or p.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are close. My sound is /d/. Everyone look up here. Some of you wrote this."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes b.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "This was our new sound we learned yesterday. This sound is /b/. We are writing /d/. This is /d/."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher writes d on the board.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is different about these two letters? Everyone think. Partner 1 tell Partner 2. Stop. Partner 2 tell Partner 1. Stop. Jesslyn, what is different about the /b/ and /d/?"
💬 **Child Response:** Child should respond that bottom bubble is different.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly, both the /d/ and /b/ have a straight line down, but one has a bubble on the back, the left, and one has a bubble in the front, on the right. Everyone say /d/. Where's the bubble, on the front or back? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** On the back.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone say /b/. Where's the bubble? Everyone."
💬 **Child Response:** On the front.

**2. Model, Explain, Engage**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "We've been working on the /b/ sound. Say the sound."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat sound.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do our sound motion. Get Ready Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children make a two-hand grab, then throw forward, saying /b/.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We can hear /b/ at the beginning of words like ball, but we can also hear /b/ at the end of words like cab."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do **Stretch the Sounds**, so we can practice how to listen for /b/ at the beginning or end."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My word is *big*. Stretch the Sounds."
💬 **Child Response:** Children say each sound /b/ /i/ /g/, while holding up a finger for every sound, then repeat the word — *big*.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My word is cab. Stretch the Sounds."
💬 **Child Response:** Children say each sound /c/ /a/ /b/, while holding up a finger for every sound, then repeat the word — cab.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher provides 4-5 words. CVC words to use: big, bin, bob, bog, bat, bed, bib, cab, job, lab, web, rib, cob.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we are going to play our **Sound Hunt Game.** You are going to stretch those sounds to hunt for the /b/ sound. You are thinking, 'Do you hear it at the beginning, or do you hear it at the end?' Each of you has a ball card because ball starts with the /b/ sound. You also have two boxes, one beginning box and one ending box."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to elkonin box on left for beginning and right for the ending.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "We are listening for where the /b/ sound is at, the beginning or ending of the word. I will say a word, and if you hear the /b/ sound at the beginning, I want you to slide the ball into the beginning box. If you hear the /b/ sound at the end of the word, I want you to slide the ball into the ending spot."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Okay. Let's get started. My turn. The word is big. Get Ready. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have moved the ball card into the beginning box.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Perfect. The word big b---i---g starts with a /b/ sound. I don't hear /b/ at the end of the word big; I hear it at the beginning. Great job."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Alright put your listening ears on. My turn. The word is *web*. Get Ready. Show me."
💬 **Child Response:** Children should have moved their ball card into the ending box.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats this process to cement sound to memory then connects to letter form.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's think about writing the /b/ sound. The letter that matches the /b/ sound -- is the letter b. Watch me closely. I will show you how to form lowercase b. No one is using their pencil yet or writing. You are watching me."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher demonstrates on a visual that shows children the correct sequence of letter strokes and uses sequence verbal cues.
✏️ **Verbal Cues for letter b:** Straight line down; put a bubble on it.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "First, start at the top line. Straight line down. Lift up. Now put a bubble on it."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats as needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Say it with me as you watch. Straight line down. Say It."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Put a bubble on it. Say it."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you say it as I write. Ready. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children repeat *straight line down, put on the belly* as teacher demonstrates the strokes.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher repeats a few times.

**3. Guided Practice**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose is to give children practice writing the letter while supporting proper pencil grip and correct sequence of stroke formation using handwriting lines."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's practice how to write lowercase b. We are not using our pencils yet. Let's do our Magic Finger Write. Place your finger at the top of the line. Let me see."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher checks students finger placement.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember it's important when we are writing to try to touch the lines. The lines are there to help us. Straight line down."
💬 **Child Response:** Children move finger from top to bottom line.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Put on a belly."
💬 **Child Response:** Children lift finger and add a bottom curve to the bottom.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Repeat as needed.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now, pick up the pencil. Show me how you hold your pencil. Remember we slant our paper. Your free hand holds the paper."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do our **Say and Write.** Say the steps as you write. Everyone put your pencil at the top line on the left. You want to start here."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to top left-hand line. Teacher corrects students if needed.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. Say and write. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter as they say the verbal cues "Straight line down. Put on the belly."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now you are ready for **Write and Sound**. You are going to write the letter b, but this time you say the sound /b/ as you write. Everyone put your pencil at the top line. Ready. Write and Sound. Go."
💬 **Child Response:** Children write the letter as they say the sound /b/.
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher monitors children's letter writing.

**4. Application**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to facilitate children's spelling of the letter or letter patterns when they hear the sound(s). This supports children's early coordination of matching sound to letter(s) for the purpose of spelling words."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Boys and girls, remember stretch the sounds. Now I am going to say a word. You are going to stretch those sounds and spell the word. We are doing three sound words. Consonant-vowel-consonant. I am going to remind you of the vowels you know so far."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher displays the a, e, i, and o vowels.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Where are we going to hear these vowels, the beginning, middle, or end of the word? Everyone. Right. These are the vowels you hear in the middle of the word. We are going to do stretch and spell. You are going to stretch the sounds of word. Look at your Stretch-Sound handwriting paper."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher directs students' attention to the handwriting sheet.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "How many sounds will we hear if these are CVC words. Everyone. Yes. Three sounds. That's why we see three boxes for each sound. You first stretch the sounds, then write the letter for each sound. Let me show you. My turn. The word is cab. So, I first stretch the sounds, c-a-b, the beginning sound is /c/. I know that the /c/ sound is written with the letter c. I am going to write c in the first block. So, we have /c/-/a/. The /a/ sound is written with the letter a, so I write the letter a, remember that's our vowel, in the middle box. So, we have /c/-/a/-/b/. That's our new sound at the end. I am going to write the letter b for the /b/ sound in the last box. Okay. Let's try."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. My first word is bin. Stretch the sound everyone. First sound. Write the letter. Second sound. Write the letter. Third sound. Write the letter."
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher uses the same list of words that were used in the Sound Hunt activity above. Teacher monitors students' answers providing corrective feedback.

**5. Wrap-Up**

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to remind students what they were focused on and provide praise for their accomplishments."

## FORM D: Scaffolded Activities

FORM D

This is FORM D.

Suggested Activities For Handwriting & Spelling

Review & Connect Activities

Tap that sound: Children can keep sticky notes with the sound on it –/a/, /s/. Before beginning a new lesson have children stick some of their sound cards on their desk. The teacher provides the sound and children tap the correct sound card. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sound Spark: The teacher points to a sound card and children must chorally pronounce the sound. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sound Detective: Children focus on listening to the beginning, middle, or end sound. The teacher says a word and indicates to children where in the word they should listen for that sound. Children then point to the correct sound. (middle-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Write the sound: The teacher says a sound, and children write the letter that corresponds to that sound on a mini whiteboard. This can also be done with writing the sound at the beginning, middle, and end for more advanced students. (high-level) (Grades: K)
How many sounds: The teacher says a word. Children use a manipulative to count and show how many sounds. This activity should be used for children at more advanced stages of phonemic awareness. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Sort the sound: The teacher says a sound and children sort by either voiced or unvoiced, continuous or stops. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)

Model, Explain, Engage Activities

Tap that Sound: Same as Review & Connect but include new targeted sound (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sound Spark: Same as Review & Connect but include new targeted sound (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sound Hunt: Teacher says words; children must listen to beginning, middle, or end sound to identify the target phoneme. Children can indicate that they hear target sound by lifting a card with the target sound. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K, 1st) — PreK: beginning sounds only. K: beginning + ending sounds. 1st: medial vowel discrimination.
Hide-and-Seek Sound: Teacher says words that have the target sound in different positions; it could be at the beginning, middle, or end position. Children must find where the sound is by holding up a beginning, middle, or end card. (middle-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Chop-a-Sound: Children are given words with the target sound. They either chop the target sound or another previously learned sound and say what is left. For example, if the target sound is /b/, children are given the bed and asked to chop the /b/, what's the word now? The answer is "ed." Likewise, children can chop another previous sound and say what's left of the word. For example, if the target sound is /b/, children are given the word bat and asked to chop /t/. What's the word now? (ba) (middle-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Sound Puzzle: Children are given two previously learned sounds at a time; they must add in the target sound to create a word. For example, if the target sound is /b/, children are given /a/ /t/; children put the sounds together to make a word/nonsense word, bat and tab. Children are then given two new sounds along with /b/ to create more words using the target sound. To increase difficulty, children are given multiple previously learned sounds. Children create as many words by incorporating the target sound. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Sound Switch-a-Roo: Children are given words that include previously learned sounds. They are asked to change a beginning, middle, or end sound by substituting in the new targeted sound. Children could also be asked to isolate the new sound and substitute with a previously learned sound. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Spell Aloud: Teacher takes previously sound patterns of words and asks children to change a sound to the target sound and spell aloud. (high-level) (Grades: 1st)
Silly Sentence: Teacher asks children to think of words that contain the target sound or sound pattern. Then collaboratively build a sentence using as many of those words as possible. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
 How Many Sounds: Teacher provides children with words that include previously learned sounds and the target sound. Children must segment the sounds in each word. Using manipulatives (e.g., chip with Elkonin boxes), children count the sounds. Children can write how many on whiteboards to display. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)

Guided Practice Activities

Rehearsal of Sequence of Strokes
Finger Run: Children run their finger from left to right across the handwriting lines, pointing to the top, middle, and bottom lines as the teacher names them. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K-struggling)
Magic Finger Write: Children use their "magic" pointer finger to trace letter shapes on the lines, following the teacher's modeling and verbal stroke cues. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sky Writing: With big arm movements, children write letters in the air while saying the strokes aloud, using shoulders and elbows to build motor memory. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Bumpy Road Letters: Children trace textured letter cards (made from sandpaper or raised paint) with their fingers, following the correct stroke path by feel. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)

Writing of the Letters/Words
Trace & Say: Children trace letters on dotted-line templates with a pencil, saying the strokes aloud (e.g., "start at the top, down, bump, back up…"). (middle-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Start Dot Letters: Letters are printed with start dots and arrows. Children follow the marks to complete each letter step-by-step. (middle-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Rainbow Write: Children trace the same letter multiple times with different colored pencils, following correct formation for each layer. (middle-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Letter Ladder: A visual with steps for each part of the letter (e.g., Step 1: Big line down; Step 2: Curve around…). Children write each step, climbing the "ladder" to finish the full letter. (middle-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Top to Bottom Challenge: Children write letters using correct formation, earning a star or check each time they start at the top and follow the correct stroke order. (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Line Talkers: Children describe how the letter touches the lines (e.g., "The b starts at the top and goes to the bottom, then the belly touches the middle line."). (high-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Cover & Try: Children trace a letter, then cover the model and try writing it on their own, focusing on correct sequence and placement. (high-level) (Grades: K-advanced, 1st)
Letter Coach: Children work in pairs—one child "coaches" by checking if the other starts in the right place and follows the stroke order, using friendly reminders. (high-level) (Grades: K-advanced, 1st)

Application Activities

Trace the Sound You Hear: Teacher says a sound, but includes multiple opportunities for children to hear the target sound (e.g., /m/ or /z/), and children trace the matching letter or letter pattern. (low-level) (Grades: PreK)
Write the Sound You Hear: Teacher says a sound (e.g., /m/ or /sh/), and children write the matching letter or letter pattern. Children can write on a handwriting sheet that displays the correct way to write previously learned letters along with the new target letter. (low-level) (Grades: PreK, K)
Sound-Letter Hunt: Teacher says words; children must listen to either the beginning, middle, or end sound to identify the target phoneme. Children then write the corresponding sound they hear at the beginning, middle, or end. Children can write on a handwriting sheet that displays the correct way to write previously learned letters along with the new target letter. For very young children in preK, use dotted lines next to the letters. (middle-level) (Grades: PreK, K, 1st) — PreK: use dotted lines, trace the letter. K: write the letter for the sound. 1st: write the full word.
Mystery Word Builder: Teacher gives sounds one at a time (e.g., /s/, /i/, /t/), and children write each sound as they hear it to form a word. Then they blend and read the completed word. (middle-level) (Grades: K, 1st)
Sound Switch Writing: Children write a simple word (e.g., mat), then change one sound when prompted (e.g., "change /m/ to /s/") to create a new word (sat). Focus should be on the target letter-sound. (high-level) (Grades: K-advanced, 1st)
What's that Pattern: Teacher provides children with words that include previously learned sounds and the target sound. Children must segment the sounds in each word. Using manipulatives (e.g., chips with Elkonin boxes), children count the sounds and then write the corresponding letter or letter patterns. (high-level) (Grades: K-advanced, 1st)

## FORM E: Evidence-Based Practices

FORM E
This is FORM E.

Major Part 1: Handwriting/Spelling Evidence-Based Practices

1. Teach the proper way to hold a pencil and write from left to right.
2. Help children pronounce, identify, and isolate phonemes correctly.
3. Teach how to form each letter representing a phoneme step by step, showing the exact order and direction of the strokes.
4. Talk with children about how letters look — compare their shapes and the strokes used to write them.
5. Have children retrieve prior phoneme-grapheme knowledge and connect to new phoneme-grapheme patterns.
6. Cue children to apply phoneme-grapheme patterns during activities.
7. Provide corrective and confirmatory feedback explaining what part is incorrect and showing them how to fix it.
8. Encourage children to use what they've learned about sounds, spelling patterns, and word parts to try spelling new words — even if they're not sure (invented spelling).

🗣️ **Teacher:**

Sound Puzzle: Children are given two previously learned sounds at a time; they must add in the target sound (either beginning, middle, or end) to create a word. For example, if the target sound is /b/, children are given /a/ /t/; children put the sounds together to make a word (or nonsense word), bat (target sound at beginning) and tab (target sound at end). Children are then given two new sounds along with /b/ to create more words using the target sound. (high-level)

Great! Now we are going to play our Sound Puzzle. We are listening for the /z/ sound at the beginning of the word. The /z/ sound is going to be the beginning sound of the word. Each of you has the /z/ sound in in front of you. I am going to give you two sounds. These are sounds you already know. You are going to put the /z/ sound and the two sounds I give you together to make a word, and it could be a nonsense word. Watch me. I will show you first. Here's two sounds: /a/ and /p/. I am thinking about ordering the sounds to make a word. I am going to place /z/ first, than /a/, then /p/. {teacher sound out the phonemes /z/ /a/ /p/}. When I say go, tell me your word. So, you would say: zap.
My turn. Your two sounds are /i/ and /p/. Put them in order. Get Ready...Go. (children should say zip). {Teacher repeats this process to cement sound to memory then connects to letter form}
