**❄️ Icy Land (Arctic / Snowy Mountains)**
**Possible Conflicts:**
A snowstorm makes it hard to find the way home.

An animal slips on the ice and gets separated from its family.

A character’s shelter collapses under heavy snow.

A character’s hands are too cold to open their supply bag.

The frozen ground cracks beneath their feet.

**🌊 Stormy Sea**
**Possible Conflicts:**
A boat gets caught in huge waves and begins to tip.

The wind blows away the map needed to find land.

Lightning strikes near the boat and breaks the mast.

A character falls overboard and needs rescue.

A storm washes a treasure chest into the deep ocean.

**🌵 Hot Desert**
**Possible Conflicts:**
A character runs out of water and gets very thirsty.

A sandstorm covers the footprints that show the way back.

A snake or scorpion blocks the path.

The sun is so hot the character has to find shade fast.

The character’s backpack breaks and they lose supplies.

**🌆 Busy City**
**Possible Conflicts:**
A child gets separated from their adult in a crowd.

A pet escapes its leash and runs into traffic.

A character loses their money and can’t get on the bus.

Loud noises scare a small child or animal.

A character’s phone dies and they can’t contact anyone.

**🌲 Deep Forest**
**Possible Conflicts:**
It gets dark and the character forgets a flashlight.

A bridge over a river breaks, and they must find another way.

A bear is blocking the path to camp.

A character drops their compass and gets lost.

Their food gets taken by a raccoon while they sleep.

EXAMPLE B: **Modification of Activity by Child Ability Level (low, middle, high) **

**#INPUT = **teacher requests a modification to increase the difficulty of the **Relevant vs. Irrelevant Match activity (low level) **

**# OUTPUT=**
***Modification Teacher Script:**
{Teacher points to the sentence on the board.} “Let’s read this topic sentence together: Penguins’ bodies help them survive the cold.” {Teacher models reading it aloud.} “That’s our main idea. Let’s look at some details. I’ll read one and you decide—does it match the main idea? Give me a thumbs up if yes, thumbs down if no, and flat if you’re not sure.” {Teacher reads: “Penguins have a thick layer of blubber under the skin.”} “Thumbs?” (Children show thumbs up) “Yes! That supports our main idea. It tells us how their body keeps them warm.” {Teacher reads: “Penguins eat krill and fish.”} “Thumbs?” (Children show thumbs down or flat) “Right. It’s about penguins, but not about how their bodies help them survive the cold. Let’s set that one aside.” {Teacher reads: “Their feathers are waterproof and trap warm air.”} “Thumbs?” (Children show thumbs up) “Great! That’s a match. It tells us more about how their bodies keep them warm.” {Teacher reads: “Penguins live in southern places like the Antarctic.”} “Thumbs?” (Children show thumbs down or flat) “Good thinking. This one is true, but not about their bodies helping with the cold.” {Teacher gestures to the board.} “Now let’s take the matching ones and put them underneath our topic sentence. That helps us stay focused and give strong support for our idea.”
