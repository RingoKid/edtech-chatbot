# Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

## A. Standards

- **CLL6.4c — Isolates the initial (beginning) sounds in words with adult guidance**
- **CLL7.4a — With prompting and support, recognizes and names some upper and lower case letters of the alphabet**
- **CLL9.4d — Writes some letters of the alphabet**

## B. Objectives

- **Orient** to the new letter **Zz** and the sound **/z/**
- **Identify** the **/z/** sound at the beginning of spoken words with adult guidance
- **Trace** uppercase **Z** and lowercase **z** using correct stroke sequence
- **Write** uppercase **Z** and lowercase **z** to represent the sound **/z/**

### 1. Review & Connect

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to start with a quick review of previously learned sounds.
 Do not use sounds that have not been directly taught. Do not use words that have sounds that have not been directly taught."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Boys and girls, we are going to start with **Tap that Sound**. We are going to review sounds we already know. Put your sound cards on your desk: **m** and **a**. My turn first. I will say a sound. You will tap the card that matches the sound. Ready... Go."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher places **m** and **a** sound cards where all children can see them and checks that students have both cards in front of them from left to right.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen: **/m/**. Tap the sound. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students tap **m**.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, **m** says **/m/**. That is correct because the sound I said was **/m/**, and the letter that matches **/m/** is **m**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen: **/a/**. Tap the sound. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students tap **a**.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, you tapped **a**. That is correct because the sound **/a/** matches the letter **a**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Let's do it again. My turn first. Listen carefully. **/m/**. Tap the sound. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students tap **m**.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good listening. **/m/** matches **m**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now listen for a new sound. My turn first. **/z/**. We are not tapping yet. Just listen. **/z/**."

🤚 **Gesture:** Wiggle index fingers like a buzzing bee.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Show me the buzzing bee fingers with me. **/z/**. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students wiggle index fingers and say **/z/**.

### 2. Model, Explain & Engage

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it's time for **Let's play Sound Hunt**. Today our new sound is **/z/**. My turn first. Listen to my mouth: **/z/**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Put your hand on your vocal box. Say **/z/** with me. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "/z/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. You felt your vocal box move because **/z/** is a sound that uses your voice."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look at my mouth. My lips are a little open. My teeth are close. My tongue is up near the top of my mouth, just behind my teeth. My voice is on: **/z/**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember that we said that the **/z/** sound is a lot like the **/s/** sound."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look at my mouth and listen to the sound of each one."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. **/z/**"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. **/s/**"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What's different?"

💬 **Child Response:** "One uses your voice." / "The **/z/** sound buzzes."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. For the **/z/** sound, our mouth is almost the same as **/s/**, with teeth close together and tongue near the top behind the teeth, but our vocal box is on and buzzing. For **/s/**, the mouth is similar, but the vocal box is off and it is quiet air."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone put your hand on your vocal box and make the **/z/** sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/z/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's make the **/s/** sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/s/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good noticing. **/z/** buzzes, and **/s/** does not."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's do our sound motion. Wiggle your buzzing bee fingers. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students wiggle index fingers and say **/z/**.

🤚 **Gesture:** Wiggle index fingers like a buzzing bee.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we are going to **Let's play Sound Hunt**. We are listening for **/z/** at the beginning of a word. If the word begins with **/z/**, hold up your **z** card. If it does not begin with **/z/**, keep your card down. My turn first."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The word is **zam**. **z-z-zam**. Does **zam** begin with **/z/**? Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "Yes." / Students hold up **z** card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, **zam** begins with **/z/**. We hear **/z/** right at the beginning, so we hold up **z**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The word is **mam**. **m-m-mam**. Does **mam** begin with **/z/**? Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "No." / Students keep card down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Correct. **mam** begins with **/m/**, not **/z/**. That is why we keep the card down."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The word is **za**. **z-z-za**. Does **za** begin with **/z/**? Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "Yes." / Students hold up card.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, **za** begins with **/z/**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "The word is **am**. **a-a-am**. Does **am** begin with **/z/**? Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "No." / Students keep card down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Correct. **am** begins with **/a/**, not **/z/**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we will connect the sound **/z/** to the letter **Z**. Watch me. No pencils yet. Eyes on the board."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher points to handwriting lines and names them while moving left to right.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "This is the top line. This is the middle line. This is the bottom line. We write from left to right. Everyone point left to right with me. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students sweep finger left to right.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good. We start on the left and move to the right."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Watch me write uppercase **Z**. My turn first. I start at the top line on the left."

✏️ **Verbal Cues:** Z – Little line across, slant down, little line across

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Uppercase **Z**. **Little line across, slant down, little line across.**"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now watch lowercase **z**. I start at the middle line on the left."

✏️ **Verbal Cues:** z – Little line across, slant down the stairs, little line across

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Lowercase **z**. **Little line across, slant down the stairs, little line across.**"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is the same about uppercase **Z** and lowercase **z**?"

💬 **Child Response:** "They both have three lines." / "They both go across, slant, across."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. That is right because both letters use the same three strokes: across, slant, across."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What is different?"

💬 **Child Response:** "Uppercase starts at the top line." / "Lowercase starts at the middle line."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Exactly. Uppercase **Z** starts at the top line. Lowercase **z** starts at the middle line."

**Corrective Feedback 1:** Some children may confuse the sound **/z/** with **/s/** because the mouth looks similar.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "You are close. Some of us said **/s/** when we need **/z/**. Listen and feel the difference."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher says **/s/**, then **/z/** while touching throat.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "This is **/s/**. My vocal box is quiet. This is **/z/**. My vocal box is buzzing. Everyone touch your throat. Say **/z/**. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "/z/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "What do you feel?"

💬 **Child Response:** "A buzz." / "It moves."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. If it buzzes, it is **/z/**. Let's say both: **/s/** ... **/z/**."

💬 **Child Response:** "/s/ ... /z/"
