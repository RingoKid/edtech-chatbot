# Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

## A. Standards
- **K.F.PA.6.a** — Isolate and pronounce initial, final, and then medial sounds in spoken words with three phonemes
- **K.F.P.1.a** — Identify and produce basic one-to-one phoneme-grapheme correspondences for the most frequently used sound for each consonant
- **K.F.P.3.a** — Use knowledge of letter/sound correspondences to connect letters (graphemes) to sounds (phonemes) to encode simple one-syllable words, including high-frequency words (VC, CV, CVC)
- **K.F.H.1.a** — Form all uppercase and lowercase letters

## B. Objectives
- **Orient** to the /b/ sound and **identify** it when it is spoken in isolation.
- **Discriminate** between /b/ and previously learned sounds.
- **Trace** and **write** uppercase **B** and lowercase **b** with correct formation and directionality.
- **Isolate** the beginning /b/ sound in simple CVC words.
- **Spell** simple CVC words by connecting sounds to letters, including **b** for /b/.

### 2. Model, Explain & Engage

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it is time for **Let's play Sound Check**. We are listening for our new sound, /b/. If you hear /b/, show me thumbs up. If it is not /b/, show me thumbs down. My turn first, then your turn."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen to how my mouth makes /b/. My lips close. Then they pop open. My voice is on. My tongue stays back. Watch me. /b/."

💬 **Child Response:** Students watch.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone put your hand on your vocal box. Say /b/. Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "/b/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. /p/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone put your hand on your vocal box and make the /b/ sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/b/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's make the /p/ sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/p/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Your turn. /b/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Thumbs up.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. Thumbs up is right because the sound I said was /b/, our new sound."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "/m/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Thumbs down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes. Thumbs down is right because /m/ is a sound we know, but it is not /b/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "/p/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Thumbs down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "/b/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Thumbs up.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "/d/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Thumbs down.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "/b/. Get ready... Go."
