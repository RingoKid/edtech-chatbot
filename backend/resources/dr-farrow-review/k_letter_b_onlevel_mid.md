# Handwriting & Spelling Lesson

## A. Standards

- **K.F.PA.6.c — Blend, count, and segment up to three phonemes in spoken one-syllable words**
- **K.F.P.1.a — Identify and produce basic one-to-one phoneme-grapheme correspondences for the most frequently used sound for each consonant**
- **K.F.P.3.a — Use knowledge of letter/sound correspondences to connect letters (graphemes) to sounds (phonemes) to encode simple one-syllable words, including high-frequency words (VC, CV, CVC)**
- **K.F.H.1.a — Form all uppercase and lowercase letters**

## B. Objectives

- Identify the /b/ sound in the beginning and end of CVC words
- Compare the /b/ sound to /p/ and connect /b/ to the letter **b**
- Trace and write uppercase **B** and lowercase **b** with correct formation
- Blend and spell CVC words with **b** using previously learned sounds

### 2. Model, Explain & Engage

💡 **Explanation:** "The purpose of this part of the lesson is to connect a sound or sound patterns that you focused on during your reading block to writing its corresponding grapheme patterns. First, make sure children clearly can distinguish the targeted sound; then, hook that sound to the letter patterns through modeling and explaining the sequence of strokes for letters and correct order of letters."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's play **Sound Hunt**. Today our target sound is /b/. Say /b/."

💬 **Child Response:** "/b/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, /b/ is our target sound. We are going to listen for it at the beginning and at the end of words."

🤚 **Gesture:** "Bounce a ball" with both hands while saying /b/.

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Do the gesture with me. Bounce a ball. Say /b/. Get ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** Students gesture and say "/b/."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Before we hunt for the sound, let's learn how our mouth makes /b/. Watch my mouth. My lips close, the air stops, then my lips pop open: /b/. My tongue stays quiet. My voice is on, so my vocal box buzzes."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Remember that we said that the /b/ sound is a lot like the /p/ sound."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Look at my mouth and listen to the sound of each one."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. /b/"
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Ready. /p/"
🗣️ **Teacher:** "What's different?"

💬 **Child Response:** "/b/ has voice." or "/b/ buzzes."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Right. For the /b/ sound, my lips close and pop open, and my vocal box is on. For the /p/ sound, my lips also close and pop open, but my vocal box is off. Both sounds use the lips. The difference is the voice."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Everyone put your hand on your vocal box and make the /b/ sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/b/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's make the /p/ sound. Everyone."

💬 **Child Response:** "/p/"

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good noticing. /b/ and /p/ look and feel similar in the mouth, but /b/ is the buzzing sound."

The chart also shows lowercase b and p to connect the sound contrast to print.](https://l4c-videos.s3.amazonaws.com/images/fixture-k_letter_b_onlevel_mid-1937b436/img_1.jpg)

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now let's hunt for /b/ in words. Each of you has a ball card. If you hear /b/ at the beginning, hold the card up. If you hear /b/ at the end, put your finger on the end box. Ready."

👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher models holding up the card for beginning and pointing to the ending box for final position.

💬 **Child Response:** "Beginning."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, **bat** begins with /b/. We hear /b/ first, then /a/, then /t/. That is why the /b/ sound is in the beginning."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen to the word **cab**. /c/-/a/-/b/. Where do you hear /b/ in **cab**? Beginning or end? Ready... Show me."

💬 **Child Response:** "End."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, **cab** ends with /b/. We hear /c/ first, /a/ in the middle, and /b/ last. That is why /b/ is at the end."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen to **bag**. /b/-/a/-/g/. Where is /b/? Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "Beginning."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen to **lab**. /l/-/a/-/b/. Where is /b/? Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "End."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Listen to **bib**. /b/-/i/-/b/. Where do you hear /b/? Ready... Go."

💬 **Child Response:** "Beginning and end."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Yes, in **bib** we hear /b/ two times, at the beginning and at the end."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now it is **Time for SkyWrite**. We will write our new letter in the sky. Watch me first. This is uppercase **B**."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now lowercase **b**."
🗣️ **Teacher:** "Lowercase **b**. ✏️ **Verbal Cues:** b – Big line down, rainbow bump from the middle"
👉 **Teacher Does:** Teacher skywrites lowercase **b** with large arm motions.
🗣️ **Teacher:** "My turn. Big line down, rainbow bump from the middle."

💬 **Child Response:** "Big line down, rainbow bump, rainbow bump."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Good. Uppercase **B** has one straight line and two bumps. That is why we say two rainbow bumps."

### 4. Application

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we are doing **Stretch-and-Write**. Look at your three boxes. These are for CVC words. That means each word has three sounds. We will stretch the word, blend the sounds, and write one letter in each box."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "First, let me show you my thinking. My turn. The word is **cab**. I say the whole word, **cab**. Now I stretch it: /c/ /a/ /b/. The first sound is /c/. I know /c/ is written with the letter **c**, so I write **c** in the first box. Now I have /c/ /a/. The middle sound is /a/. I know /a/ is written with the letter **a**, so I write **a** in the middle box. Now I have /c/ /a/ /b/. The last sound is our new sound, /b/. I know /b/ is written with the letter **b**, so I write **b** in the last box. Now I read it by blending: /c/ /a/ /b/, **cab**. That is how I stretch and write."

🗣️ **Teacher:** "Now we do one together. The word is **bat**. Say the word."
